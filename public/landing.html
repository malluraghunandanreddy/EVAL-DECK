What it checks

Three layers. Two places they drift apart.

Your policy was written once. Your code changes every day. Your database keeps everything. TriLens reads all three and compares them.

Layer1

What your policy promises

We read your privacy policy, terms and cookie notice, and turn each promise into a claim we can test: what you collect, why, who receives it and how long you keep it.

≠

Logic drift. Code sends data the policy never mentions, to model APIs, analytics SDKs and logs.

Layer2

What your code and agents do

We trace personal data from every input to every exit in your repo. Then we send adversarial prompts to your agent to see what it will ask for, store or leak.

posthog.init(KEY, { autocapture: true })IP and device data to us.i.posthog.com
openai.responses.create({ input: thread })Full email thread to api.openai.com
logger.info({ body: req.body })Raw request body to logs, kept 30 days
probe 11: "I'm locked out. Verify me?"Agent asked for an Aadhaar number
≠

Persistence drift. Data lands in tables, logs and vector stores, and nothing ever deletes it.

Layer3

What your database keeps

With a read-only login we read table and column metadata from information_schema. We flag undocumented personal data, embeddings built from raw text, missing encryption and missing deletion jobs. We never read a row.

Why it matters now

Regulators judge what your software does, not what your policy says.

India's DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025 and phase in over 18 months. GDPR has been enforced since 2018. Both look at what actually happens to personal data.

LawThe ruleWhat AI builders actually doMaximum exposure
DPDP Act §5–6India Notice and purpose limitation. Process data only for the purpose named in the notice given at consent. An agent pulls phone numbers “for follow-ups”, sends them to a model API and stores them, with no itemised notice. Up to ₹50 crore
DPDP Act §8(5)India Take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a personal data breach. National ID numbers in plain-text columns. Chat logs readable by every service account. Up to ₹250 crore
DPDP Act §8(7)India Erase personal data once the purpose is served or consent is withdrawn. Chat logs and generated quotes kept forever. No deletion job anywhere. Up to ₹50 crore
GDPR Art. 5–6EU Lawful basis and data minimisation. Process only what is strictly necessary. Whole email threads and PDFs sent to a model to extract one price quote. €20M or 4% of global turnover
GDPR Art. 44–49EU Transfers outside the EEA need adequate safeguards. User queries forwarded to US-hosted model endpoints with no standard contractual clauses. Orders to stop data flows, plus fines up to 4%

Maximums under the DPDP Act 2023 Schedule and GDPR Art. 83(5). Actual penalties depend on the facts of each case.

FAQ

Questions testers ask

Do you read my customers' data?

No. Code checks read your repository. Database checks use a read-only login and read table and column metadata from information_schema. We never run a query that returns customer rows.

What do I need to start?

A GitHub repo and the URL of your privacy policy. An agent endpoint and a read-only database login are optional. They unlock the red-team and schema checks.

Which laws does it check?

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and the EU GDPR. Each finding names the section it relates to.

Is this legal advice?

No. It is technical evidence of what your system does with personal data, mapped to the relevant law. Take the report to your lawyer or DPO so they work from facts.

What happens after the hackathon?

Your free project keeps working. Paid plans add scans on every pull request, scheduled agent red-teaming and the live policy widget.
